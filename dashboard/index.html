Reading it

The biggest leak is before anyone is asked.115 of 200 requests (57%) never reach a connector — larger than every downstream drop combined.
Once asked, the funnel is healthy-ish.65% respond, 58% of responders send the intro, 44% of intros book a meeting, 50% of meetings create an opportunity.
Status and outcomes disagree.6 of the 7 opportunity requests still show status Open/Stalled/Routed in intro_requests.csv (R1129, R1149, R1150, R1163, R1168, R1176).

Findings

The channel is noise.316 of 323 replies repeat one of 11 stock phrases; the top 7 alone are 274 replies. Only 15 replies actually offer a path.
Offers fall through the cracks.4 of the 15 offers (R1034, R1109, R1115, R1136) have no connector_asked row — $3.8M of pipeline where someone said "leave it with me" and nothing was recorded.
Delegation never lands."adding X who might know" appears 34 times; the named person was logged as asked in 0 of them. Everyone tagged is an AE / CRM owner, not a roster connector.
Silence is not a signal.43 threads got no reply, yet 18 of them were routed to a connector anyway — the ask happened outside Slack.
Slow first response.Median 27.2 h to the first reply on the 157 threads that got one.

Findings

Entity resolution is the hard part.6 groups of CRM accounts differ only by case / suffix (e.g. Ellerby Semiconductor, Inc. vs Ellerby Semiconductor), 5 of them owned by different AEs. 43 requests have no structured company and must be recovered from free text.
Connection exports need cleaning.Names with a stray leading · in every connections_*.csv; hundreds of connected_on dates pre-2020 and several in the future (up to 2028).
Inconsistent casing is systematic.requester_role, status, path_found_flag, role and account_name all mix Title / Sentence / UPPER — safe to normalise, but joins on raw strings will miss.
Outcomes are one row per request.intro_outcomes.csv has 85 rows and 85 distinct request_ids, so only one connector can ever be recorded per request.
